## Variant view
- consensus believes: December-quarter (FQ1 2027) gross margin of about 46.7%. That figure comes from Longbridge's report of consensus, and Bernstein's 46.2% estimate is described as 50 bp below consensus [S5, snippet only]. Bernstein also cites FQ1 EPS consensus of $2.92 [S5, snippet only]. AAPL closed at 340.42 on 2026-10-08, 0.2% below its 52-week closing high of 341.07 (2026-09-25), after falling 7.4% on 2026-07-31 on the July guide (prices.py history).
- where it may be wrong: memory costs rise after September and the filings flag that, while the one-time tariff help fades.
  - FQ3 2026 gross margin was 50.1%, including about 2 points from tariff refunds. Diluted EPS of $2.02 included $0.11 from tariff refunds [S1].
  - The 10-Q says the products margin rose on mix and tariff refunds, "partially offset by higher costs, including memory." It says Apple "is experiencing a period of supply constraints and increasing costs for components" including DRAM and NAND, and "expects these trends to intensify" [S2].
  - Component inventory was $7,645M on 2026-06-27, up from $2,124M on 2025-09-27 [S2]. Apple built a cost cushion, and per the call that cushion shrinks after September (CFO Parekh; Cook said market memory pricing keeps rising past September) [S4, snippet only].
  - September-quarter gross margin guidance was 47% to 48%, including about 1 point of tariff refunds. Excluding refunds, the midpoint is about 46.5% [S4, snippet only].
  - So consensus for the December quarter (about 46.7%) sits above the refund-free September-quarter midpoint (about 46.5%), even though the inventory cushion shrinks.
  - Nikkei Asia reported on 2026-10-09 that Apple cut October iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max component orders by at least 15% [S6, snippet only]. Reuters could not verify it and Apple did not comment [S6]. This is used as context only, not as a thesis input.
- the number that settles it: Apple's December-quarter gross margin guidance range on the FQ4 call. A midpoint below about 46.7% supports the idea. A midpoint at or above it ends it.

## Strongest case it is already priced in
- The memory pressure is public and quantified. Apple guided it in July, the 10-Q risk language is explicit [S2], and Bernstein's note is public [S5]. Bernstein kept Outperform [S5].
- The December quarter normally carries the highest volume, and fixed-cost leverage can lift margin from September to December. A 46.7% December figure over a refund-free 46.5% September midpoint may be reasonable for that reason alone.
- Tariff refunds may continue into the December quarter. The 10-Q says Apple has applied for refunds and books them as they are received [S2]. Amount and timing are unknown.
- Bernstein estimates international price increases run about 1.3 points above US pricing, which offsets part of the cost [S5, snippet only].
- AAPL fell only 1.1% on the Nikkei report (340.42 to 336.64, prices.py history). Nikkei production-cut reports recur most autumns.
- iPhone net sales grew 22% in FQ3 on Pro models [S2]. Demand was strong before the price increase.
